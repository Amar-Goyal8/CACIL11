Negotiators Signal Cautious Progress in Renewed Vienna Nuclear Talks

By Elena Vance

Diplomats from six nations reconvened in Vienna this week for a fresh round of negotiations over Iran's nuclear program, the first formal sitting since talks stalled last autumn. The mood, participants said, was businesslike rather than warm.

In a report circulated to member states, the International Atomic Energy Agency noted that Iran's enriched uranium stockpile reached roughly 6,200 kilograms in May, a figure one delegate described as the highest level in the program's history. Inspectors have maintained continuous access to the principal enrichment sites throughout the period.

Speaking to reporters outside the venue, an official close to the European delegation said a final agreement was "days away" from completion. Lead negotiators on both sides offered a more measured account, cautioning several technical annexes remained unresolved.

Analysts following the talks note previous rounds have produced similar signals of momentum without yielding a signed framework. A further session is expected later this month.
